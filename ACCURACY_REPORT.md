# gosgp4 accuracy report

Generated by `accuracytest/cmd/accuracycheck` in 2s.

## Methodology

- **Catalog**: `testdata/catalog.json`, a 32220-satellite Space-Track GP export (PAYLOAD/ROCKET BODY/DEBRIS/UNKNOWN, including alpha-5 catalog numbers).
- **Test engine**: `gosgp4`, `sgp4.ParseTLE(line1, line2, sgp4.WGS72, sgp4.OpsModeImproved)` then `rec.Propagate(tsince)`.
- **Two independent oracles**, compared separately below:
  1. **AGI Astro Standards** -- the official 18th Space Defense Squadron-grade SGP4 library, a *different codebase* implementing the same published theory (Hoots/Roehrich, as revisited in Vallado et al.'s 2006 "Revisiting Spacetrack Report #3"). Agreement here means two independently authored implementations agree -- the strongest form of validation.
  2. **Vallado C++ reference** -- a compiled-in copy of the *exact* `SGP4.cpp` source gosgp4 was line-for-line ported from (`reference/vallado/`). Agreement here means the port is a faithful, bug-free transcription of its own source -- a *different* (and much stricter) claim than agreeing with an independent implementation.

- **Time offsets tested** (minutes since each TLE's own epoch): t+0 (epoch), t+1h, t+6h, t+1d, t+3d, t+7d.
- **Error metric**: Euclidean distance between the two engines' ECI position (km) and velocity (km/s) vectors at each (satellite, tsince) pair.

## Plain-language summary

The tables further down report raw distances in scientific notation (km), which is hard to eyeball. This section translates the same numbers into everyday units and a percentage of the satellite's actual distance from Earth.

"Absolute worst case" is usually one specific hard orbit, not representative of overall accuracy -- see each oracle's "Worst position-error outliers" table below for exactly which satellite it is and why.
